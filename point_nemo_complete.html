What is Point Nemo?

Point Nemo is not an island or a marker floating in the sea. It is a calculated geographic location in the South Pacific Ocean, chosen because it is farther from land than any other point in the world's oceans. It is also called the oceanic pole of inaccessibility.

Where is it?

It lies in the South Pacific Ocean, between South America and New Zealand, at approximately 48°52.6′ south latitude and 123°23.6′ west longitude.

Why is it so remote?

The nearest land is thousands of kilometres away. The three closest land areas are small and remote, rather than large, populated places.

Where does the name come from?

The name “Nemo” refers to Captain Nemo, the fictional submarine captain in Jules Verne's novels. In Latin, “nemo” means “no one”.

Is there a physical point?

No special island, pole, or permanent marker identifies it. The point is a coordinate calculated from the positions of surrounding land.

The nearest land

Ducie Island

A small, uninhabited coral atoll in the Pitcairn Islands group, lying to the north of Point Nemo.

Motu Nui

A small islet near Rapa Nui (Easter Island), lying to the northeast of Point Nemo.

Maher Island

A small island near the coast of Antarctica, lying to the south of Point Nemo. These three locations are each roughly 2,688 kilometres from the oceanic pole of inaccessibility.

Why do people talk about Point Nemo?

Spacecraft re-entry region

Because it is extremely remote, a broad region of the South Pacific around Point Nemo has been used as a target area for controlled re-entry of some retired spacecraft and satellites. It is often nicknamed the “spacecraft cemetery”.

Very little nearby human activity

There are few people and relatively little regular ship traffic in this remote part of the ocean. At times, astronauts aboard the International Space Station can be among the closest humans to the area.

Ocean life

Point Nemo is in the open ocean. The surrounding waters are part of a vast marine ecosystem, even though the area is far from land and nutrient supply can be limited.

Not a place to visit casually

There is no airport, settlement, tourist facility, or public landmark at Point Nemo. Reaching the area would require a long ocean voyage.

A short history

1992 — Location calculated

Croatian-Canadian survey engineer Hrvoje Lukatela used geographic calculations and computer modelling to identify the oceanic pole of inaccessibility.

After its identification — Name becomes popular

The location became widely known as Point Nemo, using the name of Captain Nemo from Jules Verne's fiction.

Today — A geographic reference

Point Nemo is known in geography, ocean science, and discussions about controlled spacecraft re-entries into remote ocean areas.
